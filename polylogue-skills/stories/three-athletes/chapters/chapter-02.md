# Chapter 2 — *The orange jersey*

**Purpose.** A week later. Minh has received the select-team invitation off-screen and must show up to Saturday practice carrying it alone. The reader sees Minh deflect his friends, take a private phone moment, and play well but stay disconnected. The chapter ends with Ines opening her notebook — she's noticing.
**Pages:** 3.
**Comprehension check:** none (chapter focused on internal tension; no natural vocab moment that doesn't dilute the climax).
**Decision:** gate, **inferencing** (primary skill, single-detail). Group challenge.
**Routes to:** Chapter 3.

**Staging-pedagogy notes (per DECISIONS.md Section 12, step 4 finding).**
The gate asks "what's going on with Minh today?" — a single-detail inference that reads three pieces of visible evidence: (1) closed body language (deflection), (2) the private phone-check moment, (3) the missed social connection (Reuben's high-five barely returned). Each piece of evidence must be visibly staged in the panels. Wrong choices ride misreads of this evidence: surface-reading takes Minh's words at face value ("just tired"); wrong-direction reads the distance as conflict ("a disagreement with someone"). Staging must depict the evidence without editorializing — no dramatic backlighting that says "lonely," no isolation framing that says "burdened." Show the behavior; let the reader read it.

## Decision

**Skill:** inferencing (Thinking, `[gate]`).
**Kind:** gate.
**Challenge type:** group.
**Coin schedule:** 3 / 1 / 0.
**Attempts allowed:** 2.

**Prompt.** What's going on with Minh today?

**Choices.**
- (A) Minh is keeping something private — something he hasn't figured out how to share yet. *[correct]*
- (B) Minh and Coach Reyna had a disagreement. *[incorrect — `misconception_targeted: wrong-direction inference`]*
- (C) Minh is just tired — he said so. *[incorrect — `misconception_targeted: surface-reading`]*

**Hint after attempt 1 (group, discussion-prompting).** "Talk through what you saw with your group. Was Minh moving like a tired kid? Was he avoiding Coach Reyna specifically, or someone else? What was the moment with the phone telling us?"

**Why these wrongs.**

*Surface-reading* takes Minh's spoken word ("just tired") at face value and ignores the visible evidence — the phone-check, the missed eye contact with Reuben, the way he runs the drill cleanly (a tired kid's body would move differently). The hint nudges the group to compare Minh's behavior to what tired actually looks like.

*Wrong-direction inference* reads the distance correctly but mislocates its source. Minh isn't avoiding Coach Reyna specifically — he's deflecting Reuben's bid for connection, taking a private phone moment, processing something internal. The evidence pattern (phone-check, social distance from his closest friend) doesn't match a coach disagreement, which would typically show as eye-contact avoidance with the coach specifically.

The correct answer requires combining the phone-check (private communication moment) with the social distance (something unshared with friends) to land on "private, unshared, in-process."

### Skill debrief panel

Inferencing means reading what someone hasn't said. Minh kept saying "I'm fine" — but his body, his phone, the way he stopped meeting Reuben's eye were saying something different. When friends read those signs, they sometimes get the chance to be there for each other through hard, private moments. When the signs get missed, the silence keeps growing. Minh was carrying something he hadn't figured out how to share. Reading what isn't said is often how friendships stay close through what's hard to say.
