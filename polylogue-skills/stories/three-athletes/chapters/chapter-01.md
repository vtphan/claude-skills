## Comprehension check

**Skill:** vocabulary in context (Foundational, `[comprehension_check]`).
**Challenge type:** individual.
**Coin schedule:** 1 / 0 / 0 (first-attempt correct = 1 coin; second-attempt or failed = 0).
**Attempts allowed:** 2.

**Prompt.** What does Reuben mean when he says the fence guy might be "scoping out new recruits"?

**Choices.**
- (A) Looking at the kids through a telescope or binoculars. *[incorrect — `misconception_targeted: surface-of-the-word`]*
- (B) Watching to see which kids he might want to add to a team. *[correct]*
- (C) Examining each kid carefully up close, one at a time. *[incorrect — `misconception_targeted: ignoring context`]*

**Hint after attempt 1 (individual, text-anchored).** "Look at the rest of Reuben's sentence — 'scoping out new recruits *for his rec league*.' Is he picturing the man looking *closely*, or looking *for* something specific?"

**Why these wrongs.** "Surface-of-the-word" treats *scope* as the literal optical instrument; "ignoring context" picks the dictionary meaning of "scoping" (close visual examination) without registering that the surrounding phrase ("for his rec league") makes it figurative. The correct answer requires using the full phrase as context.
