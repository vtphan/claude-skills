# Chapter 3 — *The second field*

**Purpose.** A week later. Ines, on a Saturday-afternoon errand for her grandmother, walks past Maplewood Park and sees a kid in a bright orange jersey on a field she doesn't usually pass. She watches long enough to see the silver chain catch the light. She overhears the coach mention next-week tryouts. She doesn't call out. Chapter ends with Ines on her front steps, notebook open, fragments laid out — the moment of multi-detail inferencing for the reader's group.

## Decision

**Skill:** inferencing (Thinking, `[gate]`) — multi-detail variant.
**Kind:** gate.
**Challenge type:** group.
**Coin schedule:** 3 / 1 / 0.
**Attempts allowed:** 2.

**Prompt.** Putting together what Ines just saw at Maplewood Park with what we noticed at last Saturday's practice — the phone, the way Minh said he was "just tired" — what's the most supported reading?

**Choices.**
- (A) Minh quit playing with us and joined that other team instead. *[incorrect — `misconception_targeted: surface-reading`. Reads only what Ines saw at the second field. Misses last Saturday's evidence — the phone-checking, the deflection — which suggests something kept hidden, not openly switched.]*
- (B) Minh has been invited to try out for a more competitive team and hasn't told us. *[correct.]*
- (C) Something is going wrong in Minh's family, and he's been pulled into a different program because of it. *[incorrect — `misconception_targeted: wrong-direction`. Imports a non-soccer frame (family difficulty) when the evidence is soccer-specific (orange jersey, tryouts, select team). Reads the secrecy correctly but misplaces its source.]*

**Hint after attempt 1 (group, discussion-prompting).** "Talk through both Saturdays with your group. What was the moment with the phone last week? What did Coach Park say next to the fence today? Try saying out loud what each piece is, and what story they tell when you put them together."

**Why these wrongs.**

*Surface-reading* fixates on the most striking single piece of evidence — Minh in a different team's jersey at a different field — and reads it at face value: he switched teams. It misses last Saturday's evidence pattern (the phone moment, the deflection from Reuben). The hint redirects the group to put both Saturdays together.

*Wrong-direction* recognizes the secrecy correctly (something is being hidden) but locates its source in a non-soccer frame (family trouble). It misses the soccer-pipeline-specific evidence — *select team*, *tryouts*, *orange jerseys*. The hint redirects to what Coach Park actually said.

The correct answer requires combining: (1) the visible new-team evidence from this chapter, (2) the verbal "select team / tryouts" context overheard at the fence, and (3) the secrecy pattern from Chapter 2's phone moment and deflection. Three-piece inference, mastery sign G6.

### Skill debrief panel

Inferencing isn't always about a single moment. Sometimes the strongest inference is the one you build by laying small pieces side by side: the phone-check from one Saturday, the orange jersey from another, the words you overheard at a fence. Each piece on its own can be misread or shrugged off. Together, they tell a story you can act on. Ines saw something important. So did the group last week. But what got missed was the chance to put them together — and once that chance passed, Minh kept carrying it alone, and the silence between three friends grew quietly into distance.
