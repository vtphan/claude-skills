# Literature red flags

Use this file in Phase 1 to place the paper in its proper conversation and to detect omissions that are serious enough to affect the review.

## General red flags

- The paper frames a problem as novel even though it is obviously longstanding in the field.
- The related work section cites only the most recent adjacent trend.
- The paper claims novelty where the real contribution is narrower, such as applying an existing idea in a new setting.

## AI in education

- The paper cites only recent LLM or generative-AI papers and skips earlier AIED or learning-sciences work.
- The paper equates conversational fluency with pedagogical quality.
- The paper discusses engagement or satisfaction as if that were evidence of learning.

## Learning analytics / EDM

- The paper treats logged behavior as a transparent measure of learning without defending the construct.
- The methodological contribution is thin and the educational contribution is also thin.
- The paper prioritizes prediction accuracy without showing why the prediction matters or what action it enables.

## Theory / framework papers

- The framework renames familiar constructs without explaining what is gained.
- The paper synthesizes literature selectively, omitting major competing frameworks.
- The argument is broad and ambitious, but the cited literature base is narrow or recent-only.

## Tools and systems papers

- The paper presents a system as novel while ignoring prior tools or established workflow patterns.
- The evaluation speaks only to user enthusiasm, not to the central system claim.

## Position papers

- The argument does not engage the strongest opposing view.
- The claims are normative but the paper does not acknowledge tradeoffs.

## How to use this file

Do not turn every red flag into a criticism. Use it to decide:

- what literature tradition the paper belongs to
- whether a perceived gap is recommendation-pivotal
- whether the criticism is about novelty, framing, or scholarly grounding
