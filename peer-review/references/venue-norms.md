# Venue norms for CS Ed and AI-in-education reviewing

This file encodes durable character: what each venue prioritizes, what methodological expectations are typical, and what kinds of contributions the venue is built for. It does not encode current AI-assistance policies for reviewers, because those policies change and vary by year and by track. Whenever the user names a venue, remind them once that they should verify the venue's current AI-assistance policy directly before using this skill. After that reminder, proceed.

When characterizing a paper for a venue, treat these norms as defaults, not rules. Authors sometimes submit papers that fit a different venue's norms; that itself is reviewable information.

## SIGCSE Technical Symposium

Practitioner-research hybrid, leaning practitioner. The audience is computing educators. Papers are evaluated on relevance to teaching practice, clarity, and the likelihood that other instructors can use what is reported. Methodological rigor matters but is calibrated to the venue: a single-section experience report with thoughtful reflection is acceptable if positioned that way; the same paper claiming generalizable causal effect would be reviewed harder. Replicability of the teaching intervention (can another instructor try this?) is often more important than statistical power. Multi-section, multi-institution work is welcome but not required.

Common paper types: experience reports, curriculum descriptions, tool papers with classroom evaluation, small empirical studies. Position papers and panels exist as separate tracks.

## ITiCSE

Similar to SIGCSE in audience but with a stronger international and research orientation. Working group reports are a distinguishing feature. Methodological expectations are slightly higher than SIGCSE but the practitioner relevance is still core. The European context shapes some conventions — e.g., assessment cultures and curricular structures vary.

## ICER

The most research-oriented of the major CS Ed venues. Methodological rigor is expected and reviewed seriously. Theoretical grounding matters: papers should connect to learning sciences, education research, or computing-education theory rather than only to recent technical work. Qualitative work is welcome and held to qualitative-research standards (saturation, member checking, reflexivity, theoretical framing). Quantitative work is held to quantitative standards (power, validated measures, effect sizes, threats to validity). Replications and theory-building work are valued. ICER is the venue where "single-section, instructor-as-researcher, no comparison" papers face the hardest reviewing.

## Computers and Education Open

Open-access journal, broader than CS Ed — includes educational technology generally. Empirical work is the main currency. Journal-length: papers are expected to be more thorough than conference papers, with more developed literature reviews, methods, and discussions. Multi-study papers are common.

## Koli Calling

Smaller, specialized CS Ed conference with a strong research orientation, similar in character to ICER but with more tolerance for in-progress and exploratory work. Discussion at the venue is part of the review experience — papers that open productive conversations are valued.

## CompEd

ACM's CS Ed conference focused on regions outside North America/Europe. Reviewing should weight relevance to the regional contexts the venue serves. Methodological expectations are similar to SIGCSE, with extra credit for engagement with educational realities in under-represented contexts.

## AIED (Artificial Intelligence in Education)

Long-established AI-in-education conference, predates the LLM era. Expects engagement with the AIED literature on intelligent tutoring systems, learner modeling, pedagogical agents, open learner models, and related traditions. A common reviewing concern: papers that treat the field as if it began with ChatGPT and ignore decades of relevant prior work. Strong methodological expectations for both AI components (does the AI do meaningful work?) and educational evaluation (is learning actually measured?).

## EDM (Educational Data Mining)

Methodology-forward. Papers are expected to engage seriously with the EDM/learning-analytics methods literature. Datasets, models, and analytic techniques are first-class contributions. Papers that apply off-the-shelf methods to a CS Ed dataset without methodological contribution face higher bars on the educational side. Reproducibility (shared data, code, prompts) is increasingly expected.

## LAK (Learning Analytics and Knowledge)

Closely related to EDM but with stronger emphasis on actionable insights for teaching and learning, ethical implications, and engagement with theory beyond methodology. The conference takes ethical concerns about analytics — surveillance, consent, equity — seriously, and reviewers do too.

## L@S (Learning at Scale)

Focused on learning at scale: MOOCs, large-enrollment courses, online platforms with many learners. Papers are expected to leverage scale in their methods or claims; small-sample work, however well done, is usually not a fit. Strong overlap with LAK and EDM in methods.

## Generic CS Ed reviewing norms (when venue is not on file)

If the venue is not in this file, ask the user once for: practitioner / research / hybrid orientation, methodological expectations (qualitative welcome? large-n expected?), policy on AI assistance for reviewers, and any venue-specific concerns. Use those for the review and offer the user a one-paragraph addition to this file at the end.

## What to do with these norms in Phase 1

Read the venue's entry before writing the characterization. The entry should shape the "likely impact" paragraph and inform which weaknesses are foregrounded as conditional on the venue. For example, "single-instructor, no comparison" reads differently for SIGCSE than for ICER; the characterization should reflect that.
