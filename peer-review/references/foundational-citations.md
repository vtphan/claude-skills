# Foundational citation prompts

This file is not a bibliography to quote from mechanically. It is a reminder to check whether a paper appears to know the older conversation it is entering.

## CS education broadly

Look for engagement with the longstanding CS Ed literature when the paper makes broad claims about novice learning, misconceptions, assessment, peer instruction, programming process, or inclusive pedagogy. The exact citations vary by topic; the point is to notice when a paper behaves as if no prior literature exists outside the latest subfield trend.

## AI in education

For AIED-facing papers, check whether the paper engages with work on intelligent tutoring systems, pedagogical agents, learner modeling, feedback, self-regulated learning support, and classroom deployment. A recent LLM paper that cites only other recent LLM papers is often under-grounded.

## Learning analytics and EDM

For LAK and EDM papers, check whether the paper engages with prior work on construct validity, trace interpretation, prediction versus intervention, ethics, and actionability, not only benchmark performance.

## Theory and frameworks

For theoretical papers, check whether the paper cites the foundational theories or frameworks it builds on, revises, or rejects. Missing adjacent work matters less than missing the central intellectual ancestors of the argument.

## How to use this file

Only raise a literature-gap criticism when it is recommendation-relevant:

- the missing literature makes the claimed novelty doubtful
- the missing literature weakens the argument or framework
- the omission causes the paper to miss obvious design or methodological concerns

Do not bluff citations. If the user knows the sub-literature better, let them calibrate the severity.
