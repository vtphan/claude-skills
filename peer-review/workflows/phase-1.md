# Phase 1 — Comprehension and conceptual merit

The deepest phase. Loaded when Phase 1 begins. Output is dense and citation-rich; expect two or three turns.

Phase 1 produces *findings*, not strengths or weaknesses. Strength/weakness is a venue-relative judgment assigned in Phase 4b.

Use paper-type rubric themes lightly in this phase if helpful for classification or for spotting likely conceptual or design pressure points. Do not run a full rubric walkthrough here; full rubric application is reserved for Phase 3.

## Reading the paper

Read end to end before producing output. If only metadata is available (title or abstract pasted), say so and ask for the full paper — Phase 1 cannot proceed from an abstract.

## Pacing

Phase 1 unfolds in two turns by default:

- **Turn 1:** classification, what the authors did, the claims list. End by asking the user to confirm or edit the claims list. Wait.
- **Turn 2:** intellectual move, conceptual coherence, then 1–3 closing questions and the off-ramp.

Do not collapse turn 1 into turn 2. The claims-list confirmation is the Phase 1 → Phase 3 handoff and must be settled before the conceptual assessment is built on top of it.

## 1. Paper-type classification

One short paragraph. Name the type and the rubric(s) Phase 3 will load: empirical CS Ed, tools/systems, AI-in-education intervention, theoretical/framework, position/argument, replication, learning analytics, or mixed/unclear with explanation. If the paper is mixed (e.g., a tool with empirical evaluation), name both rubrics. Announce so the user can correct.

## 2. What the authors did

The deepest section. A neutral description the authors would recognize as accurate, even if they disagreed with later evaluation. Cover:

- The research question or design problem pursued.
- The method, intervention, system, or argument as executed.
- The setting: population, course context, scale, instructor role, duration.
- What was actually done, not what was planned.

Cite section numbers throughout. Do not paraphrase the abstract — the abstract is the authors' framing; Phase 1 needs the work itself.

For theoretical or argumentative papers, describe the argument's structure and moves rather than empirical execution.

## 3. What the authors claim

A numbered list. Each item:

- Phrased as the authors phrase it, not as the LLM's interpretation.
- Cited (typically abstract, §1, §6, §7).
- Categorized informally: contribution (what's new), empirical (what was found), normative (what should change), or generalizability (where it applies).

Aim for 3–8 claims. Flag any claim that appears in the abstract or conclusion but is not supported in the body — that is itself a finding for Phase 3 to test.

End turn 1 by asking the user to confirm or edit the list. The confirmed list is the Phase 1 → Phase 3 handoff.

## 4. Intellectual move and rationale

Why these claims would matter if true. What conceptual work the paper is doing — what conversation it intervenes in, what existing position it sharpens or contests, what gap it fills.

This is the *setup* for a relevance judgment, not the judgment itself. Treat it as: "if these claims hold, here is what the paper is doing intellectually." The relevance judgment is Phase 2.

Light literature touch: name the tradition the paper appears to be part of. Do not exhaust the literature engagement here.

## 5. Conceptual coherence

A coherence finding, citation-grounded:

- Are the key constructs defined and distinct, or do they blur together (cite where)?
- Is the argument internally consistent?
- Does the work-as-described in §2 actually instantiate the claimed contribution in §3? This is the most important check — the gap between what was done and what is claimed about it is recoverable here, before any venue lens is applied.

State findings plainly. "The construct distinction between X and Y is blurred at §2.3" is a finding; it is not yet a weakness.

## Closing questions

Surface 1–3 questions where the user's judgment is needed. Tag each as **recommendation-pivotal** or **shaping**.

Format per question:

- The question, with paper citations.
- The LLM's tentative read and the strongest counter-reading.
- What answer or evidence would shift the LLM's view.

Default to the smallest useful set. If two questions resolve most of the uncertainty, ask two.

## Off-ramp

After the closing questions, offer:

> Ready to move to Phase 2 (relevance to the field), skip to Phase 4 (synthesis and draft), or stop here?

If the user stops, end the session without a draft. If they skip to Phase 4, carry forward what Phase 1 produced; Phase 4 works with what is available.

## What this phase outputs to working state

- Paper classification (and the rubric(s) Phase 3 will load).
- The confirmed claims list.
- Phase 1 findings: the intellectual-move read and the coherence verdict, both citation-grounded.
- Any venue-sensitive flags surfaced in Phase 1, attached to the finding they qualify.
- Open Phase 1 questions, tagged pivotal or shaping.
