# Calibration

Two checks run before the review is final: a self-check the skill performs silently before handing the draft to the user, and a reviewer self-check the skill puts to the user.

## Skill's self-check (silent, before presenting the draft)

Run through these questions internally. If any answer is "no" or "uncertain," revise the draft before presenting it.

- **Are claims supported by cited evidence?** Walk the strengths and weaknesses sections. Does each evaluative claim have a section, figure, or page citation? Does the cited location actually support the claim? Re-check at least the most consequential citations.
- **Would a thoughtful author find any criticism unfair or based on misreading?** This is the single highest-leverage check. If a criticism could be answered by "actually we addressed that in §X," either the section was missed in Phase 1 or the criticism is wrong. Re-read the paper for any criticism that feels strong; confirm the paper does not already address it.
- **Would a thoughtful program chair find the reasoning solid?** The recommendation should be auditable. The chain from observed facts to recommendation should be visible in the draft.
- **Is the recommendation consistent with the accumulated weight of evidence?** Walk the reasoning trail. If the trail logs three lean-shifts toward acceptance and one toward rejection, and the final recommendation is rejection, the trail should explain why. If it cannot, the recommendation may be miscalibrated.
- **Is the conclusion consistent with what the authors said they were doing?** Re-read the abstract and intro fresh. If the recommendation is "the contribution is incremental" and the authors framed the contribution modestly to begin with, that may be unfair — the recommendation should match the standard the authors implicitly invoked.

If revisions result, make them silently before presenting the draft. Do not narrate the self-check to the user.

## Reviewer self-check (with the user, after presenting the draft)

After presenting the draft, ask the user two or three of these questions. Choose the ones most likely to surface miscalibration given the conversation. Do not ask all of them every time.

- **Are you reviewing this paper or the paper you wish had been written?** Especially relevant if the iteration repeatedly returned to "the authors should have done X instead." The standard is whether what they did is good, not whether what they wish they had done would have been better.
- **Are you holding it to standards appropriate to the venue?** Especially relevant if the lean drifted toward harshness during iteration on methodological points that may be over-weighted for this venue.
- **Are you being harsher or more lenient than usual, and why?** Sometimes the answer is legitimate (the authors are senior and should know better; the topic is one the user has strong views on; the user reviewed a similar paper recently and is calibrating against it). Sometimes the answer reveals bias.
- **Is there anything in the paper you noticed but did not raise during iteration?** Sometimes a strength or weakness is felt but not articulated. The draft should reflect what the user actually sees, not only what they put into words.
- **Does the recommendation feel right when you read it?** The simplest gut-check. If the reasoned recommendation and the gut feeling diverge, that is worth interrogating before submitting.

Ask the questions one at a time, in conversation. Wait for each answer before asking the next. If an answer suggests the draft needs revision, offer to revise.

## When the user's self-check answer changes something

If the user says "actually, I am being harsher than usual because [reason]," do not assume the draft must soften — the harshness may be warranted. Surface the tradeoff: "Want me to soften the criticism in [section], keep it but add the reasoning to the recommendation, or leave it as is?"

If the user says "I noticed the discussion section is unusually thoughtful and I should have raised that as a strength," add it. The draft is not finished until the user signs off.

## Closing the review

Once the user is satisfied with the draft, the skill's job is done. Do not summarize the conversation, do not offer additional analysis, do not ask if there is anything else. The user can come back if they want changes; otherwise the review is finished.

If the venue was not on file in `references/venue-norms.md` and the skill asked for norms during Phase 1, offer the one-paragraph addition for the user's file at this point.
