# Rubric: replication papers

Use this rubric when the paper explicitly reproduces, replicates, or re-tests prior work.

Use this rubric in Phase 1 after classification. It should shape the paper assessment, claim/evidence verdicts, and question selection; it is not a checklist to walk mechanically. In Phase 2, use its findings only through the venue-lens assessment.

## Replication target

- Is the original study or claim clearly identified?
- Does the paper explain what kind of replication this is: direct, close, conceptual, or partial?
- Is the motivation for replicating this study persuasive?

## Fidelity and deviation

- Does the paper document what was kept the same and what changed?
- Are deviations from the original study justified and consequentiality discussed?
- Is the replication faithful enough that the result is interpretable?

## Interpretation

- If results differ from the original, are alternative explanations considered carefully?
- Does the paper avoid overclaiming from a failed or partial replication?
- If results align, does the paper articulate what confidence that adds?

## Context and generalizability

- Does the new context matter: institution, population, course level, time, platform, or instructor?
- Does the paper explain whether differences are due to context or to methodological drift?
- Are claims calibrated to what replication can actually establish?

## Reporting

- Is the original procedure documented well enough for readers to evaluate fidelity?
- Are materials, measures, and analysis choices transparent?
- Does the paper contribute to cumulative knowledge rather than merely re-running a study?

## Common review pivots

Questions often worth escalating to Phase 2:

- Is the replication target important enough to justify the paper?
- Are the deviations too large for the paper to count as a meaningful replication?
- Does the paper interpret divergence from the original too aggressively?
