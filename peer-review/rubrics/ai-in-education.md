# Rubric: AI-in-education papers

Use this rubric for papers where an AI component is central to the contribution. Pair it with an empirical, tools/systems, or theoretical rubric as needed.

Use rubric themes lightly in Phases 1–2 if helpful for classification, literature-positioning, or question selection. Apply the rubric in full in Phase 3.

## Educational problem and theory of change

- What educational problem is the AI supposed to improve?
- Is there a plausible mechanism linking the AI behavior to learning, teaching, feedback quality, or instructor efficiency?
- Is the paper grounded in educational goals rather than only model capability?

## AI contribution

- What is actually novel in the AI component: prompting, orchestration, learner modeling, feedback policy, adaptation logic, or system integration?
- Is the AI contribution meaningful, or would a simple non-AI baseline likely do nearly the same work?
- Are important implementation choices described clearly enough to evaluate?

## Educational evaluation

- Are learning outcomes measured when the paper makes learning claims?
- If the paper measures satisfaction, perceived usefulness, or engagement, are claims kept at that level?
- Are the evaluation tasks authentic to the learning setting the paper claims to address?

## Baselines and comparisons

- Are there meaningful human, non-AI, or simpler-AI comparison points?
- If there is no comparison, is the paper framed modestly enough?
- Are claims about superiority matched to the available evidence?

## Safety, ethics, and failure modes

- Does the paper address hallucination, harmful feedback, bias, privacy, surveillance, over-reliance, or academic-integrity risks where relevant?
- Are the failure cases concrete, or only gestured at?
- Is there discussion of when the AI should not be used?

## Literature grounding

- Does the paper engage with pre-LLM AIED, ITS, pedagogy, or learning-sciences work where relevant?
- Does it acknowledge adjacent work on feedback, tutoring, learner modeling, or classroom deployment?
- Is the contribution framed as part of an existing research tradition rather than as if the field began recently?

## Common review pivots

Questions often worth escalating to Phase 2:

- Is the AI component genuinely necessary?
- Are the paper's educational claims too strong for the measures used?
- Is the literature grounding adequate for an AIED-facing venue?
