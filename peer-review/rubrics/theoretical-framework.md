# Rubric: theoretical and framework papers

Use this rubric when the paper's main contribution is conceptual rather than empirical: a framework, model, taxonomy, theory integration, conceptual lens, or explanatory architecture.

## Core constructs

- Are the key constructs defined clearly and consistently?
- Are important terms distinguished from nearby concepts, or do they blur together?
- Could another researcher or designer apply the constructs without guessing what the authors mean?

## Internal coherence

- Do the parts of the framework fit together logically?
- Are relationships among constructs explicit rather than implied?
- Are scope conditions and assumptions stated clearly?

## Novelty and intellectual contribution

- Is the framework genuinely new, or mainly a relabeling, synthesis, or re-packaging?
- If it is a synthesis, is that synthesis itself valuable enough to justify publication?
- Does the paper resolve a real ambiguity, fragmentation, or blind spot in the field?

## Explanatory and analytic power

- Does the framework explain phenomena better than existing alternatives?
- Does it sharpen how future empirical or design work should be done?
- Can it discriminate among cases, or is it broad enough to fit everything?

## Operationalizability

- Could the framework guide study design, data collection, coding, intervention design, or interpretation?
- Are there examples showing how one would use it in practice?
- If the paper makes no empirical claims, does it still make testable or practically useful commitments?

## Literature grounding

- Does the paper engage with foundational and adjacent theory rather than only recent related work?
- Does it fairly represent the traditions it synthesizes or critiques?
- Are important predecessor frameworks or competing perspectives missing?

## Limits and boundary conditions

- Does the paper state where the framework should and should not apply?
- Are unresolved tensions acknowledged?
- Does the paper avoid universalizing claims that exceed its argument?

## Common review pivots

Questions often worth escalating to Phase 2:

- Is the framework sufficiently novel for this venue?
- Is the paper making a real theoretical advance or only organizing familiar ideas?
- Is the framework actionable enough to matter beyond a well-written discussion section?
