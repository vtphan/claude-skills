# Rubric: position and argument papers

Use this rubric when the paper's contribution is primarily an argument, agenda, or normative claim rather than a new framework or new empirical evidence.

Use this rubric in Phase 1 after classification. It should shape the paper assessment, claim/evidence verdicts, and question selection; it is not a checklist to walk mechanically. In Phase 2, use its findings only through the venue-lens assessment.

## Central thesis

- Is the main claim clear, contestable, and worth arguing about?
- Does the paper know what it is trying to persuade the field to believe or do?

## Structure of the argument

- Is the reasoning coherent and cumulative?
- Are major premises supported by evidence, examples, or prior work?
- Does the paper avoid rhetorical leaps?

## Scholarly engagement

- Does it engage seriously with the strongest opposing views?
- Are the cited literatures broad enough for the claim's ambition?
- Does it acknowledge where the field is genuinely divided?

## Value to the venue

- Will this argument sharpen debate, guide practice, or redirect research in a useful way?
- Is the claim timely and specific enough for the audience?
- Does the paper read like a genuine scholarly intervention rather than an opinion essay?

## Limits

- Are the paper's recommendations bounded, or does it overgeneralize from selective evidence?
- Are counterexamples or failure cases discussed?

## Common review pivots

Questions often worth escalating to Phase 2:

- Is the thesis strong enough to justify a position paper at this venue?
- Does the paper engage opposing views fairly?
- Is the paper actually a framework paper and better reviewed with a theoretical rubric instead?
